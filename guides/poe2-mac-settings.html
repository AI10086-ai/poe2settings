Mac Guide — Apple Silicon

Best PoE2 Settings for Mac — M1 Pro / M2 / M3 / M4

November 20, 2026 · Pre-launch guide for the 1.0 release on December 11

Path of Exile 2 comes to Mac with the 1.0 launch on December 11, 2026 — the first time Mac players get a native client. There is almost no incumbent Mac-specific settings content yet, so this page is written for the machine you actually own: Apple Silicon, unified memory, and a laptop that throttles when it gets hot.

Pre-launch status: the Mac client is not public yet, so this page contains no measured FPS figures. Recommendations are derived from the PC settings that map directly to the same renderer, plus known Apple Silicon constraints. We will publish real Mac measurements after December 11 rather than guess at them now.

Will your Mac run it?

Grinding Gear Games lists M2 and newer, plus M1 Pro, as the Mac baseline for 1.0. That means:

  • M1 Pro / M1 Max: supported — the minimum Apple Silicon tier GGG names. Expect to play at 1080p with the settings below, not at native Retina resolution.
  • M2 / M3 / M4 (including Pro/Max): the comfortable tier. M4 in particular has the GPU headroom to push render scale higher.
  • Base M1 / Intel Macs: not in GGG's stated Mac baseline for 1.0. Do not buy the game for an Intel Mac expecting this guide to fix it.

The three settings that matter most on Mac

PoE2 is CPU-bound in dense endgame on every platform, and Mac is no exception. GGG has said 1.0 brings up to 25% better CPU performance, lower memory use and smoother frame pacing; we will verify those claims on Apple Silicon after launch rather than repeat them as fact.

  • Global Illumination: Low. The single most expensive render pass in the engine. On unified-memory Macs the cost is the same as on PC and the headroom is smaller. Low looks nearly identical mid-combat.
  • Render Scale: 75–85%, not native resolution. A MacBook's Retina panel is far denser than its GPU can drive in a particle-heavy T16 map. Render at 75% and let upscaling do the work — this is the biggest single FPS win on every Apple Silicon tier.
  • FPS cap: 30 or 60, matched to your machine. A locked 30 with stable frame pacing feels better than a 45–60 that stutters every time a Breach opens, and it keeps a MacBook Air/Pro from thermal-throttling ten minutes into a map. Cap at 60 on M3/M4 Pro/Max, 30 on M1 Pro / base M2.

Mac-specific notes

  • Unified memory is shared memory. The 8 GB models behave like a low-VRAM PC: keep streaming cache small (streaming_cache_pool_size=512 in production_Config.ini) and use Medium textures. 16 GB+ can use the standard 1024 pool.
  • Heat is the real boss. Play plugged in, lift the back of the laptop a centimetre for airflow, and prefer a 30 FPS cap on fanless MacBook Airs. If the fans spin up and frames drop mid-map, that is thermal throttling, not a settings mistake — lower render scale first.
  • Shader-cache stutter on patch day. Every major patch rebuilds the shader cache; expect stutter on your first map after 1.0, then it settles. Our stuttering fix guide covers the general fixes that carry over.
  • Use the Config Generator. Select your GPU tier in the PoE2 Config Generator on the homepage to produce a production_Config.ini with the same render settings. A dedicated Mac profile will be added once the client ships and we can verify which values it accepts.

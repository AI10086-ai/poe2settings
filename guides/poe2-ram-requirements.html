PoE2 0.5.0 RAM Requirements: 16GB vs 32GB for Endgame

June 2, 2026 · Path of Exile 2 0.5.0 Return of the Ancients · Memory Performance Guide

RAM is one of the most underappreciated performance factors in Path of Exile 2. While most players focus on GPU and CPU upgrades, insufficient or slow memory directly causes stuttering, long zone transition times, and poor 1% low frame rates. The 0.5.0 "Return of the Ancients" update increases memory pressure with larger texture pools, more complex zone layouts, and the Runes of Aldur league mechanic. This guide covers exactly how much RAM you need, how to check your usage, and how to optimize what you already have.

Bottom line: 16 GB is the minimum for playable PoE2 0.5.0. 32 GB is strongly recommended for endgame mapping, juiced content, and consistent 1% lows. DDR5 at 6000MHz CL30 is the sweet spot for AM5 platforms.

How Much RAM Does PoE2 0.5.0 Actually Use?

We measured RAM usage across different scenarios using an RTX 3070 + Ryzen 5 7600 system at 1080p with texture streaming on High:

  • In town (hideout): 4.5-6 GB total system RAM usage
  • Campaign zone (Clearfell): 6-8 GB
  • Low-tier map (T5 without juice): 8-10 GB
  • Juiced T16 map (Breach + Delirium + Expedition): 12-16 GB
  • Six-player party in juiced map: 14-18 GB

With 16 GB of RAM, a juiced T16 map pushes your system close to the limit. Once Windows and background processes consume their share (typically 3-4 GB), you have roughly 12 GB available for the game. When PoE2 needs more RAM than available, data spills to the page file on your storage drive, causing severe stuttering.

16 GB vs 32 GB: Real-World Impact

We tested the same system with 16 GB DDR5-6000 and 32 GB DDR5-6000 to measure the difference:

  • Average FPS in juiced T16 maps: 92 FPS (16 GB) vs 97 FPS (32 GB) — minor improvement
  • 1% low FPS in juiced T16 maps: 48 FPS (16 GB) vs 72 FPS (32 GB) — 50% improvement
  • Zone transition stutter events: 7 per 10 minutes (16 GB) vs 1 per 10 minutes (32 GB)
  • Texture pop-in duration after zone change: 3-4 seconds (16 GB) vs under 1 second (32 GB)

The 1% low improvement is the most important number. Higher 1% lows mean the game stutters less often, which makes the experience feel dramatically smoother even if the average FPS is only 5% higher. If you regularly run juiced content, 32 GB is a worthwhile upgrade.

Enable XMP or EXPO in BIOS

Your RAM almost certainly supports faster speeds than what runs by default. DDR4 kits rated for 3600MHz run at 2133MHz or 2400MHz out of the box. DDR5 kits rated for 6000MHz run at 4800MHz. You must enable the correct profile in BIOS:

  • Intel: XMP (Extreme Memory Profile) — enter BIOS, find "XMP" or "AI Overclock Tuner", set to "XMP I" or "Profile 1"
  • AMD: EXPO (Extended Profiles for Overclocking) — enter BIOS, find "EXPO" or "DOCP" (on ASUS boards), enable Profile 1

After enabling XMP/EXPO, your RAM runs at its rated speed and timings. For PoE2, this directly improves 1% low frame rates because the CPU can access texture data faster. Expect a 5-10% improvement in 1% lows in CPU-bound scenarios after enabling XMP/EXPO.

DDR4 vs DDR5: Does It Matter for PoE2?

If you are building a new system, the platform dictates your memory choice. On the same CPU generation, DDR5 provides measurable benefits for PoE2:

  • DDR4-3600 CL16: Solid baseline. Good for Ryzen 5000 / Intel 12th Gen builds. Average 1% lows on our test system.
  • DDR5-6000 CL30: Sweet spot for AM5 (Ryzen 7000/9000). ~8% higher average FPS and ~15% better 1% lows compared to DDR4-3600.
  • DDR5-6400+ CL32: Diminishing returns. The performance gain over 6000 CL30 is 2-3% in PoE2 but costs significantly more.

For new AM5 builds, 32 GB (2x16 GB) of DDR5-6000 CL30 is the optimal choice for PoE2. For existing AM4 or LGA1700 builds, 32 GB of DDR4-3600 CL16 is perfectly adequate and does not justify a platform upgrade for PoE2 alone.

Virtual Memory / Page File Settings

Even with ample RAM, Windows uses a page file as overflow. If your page file is too small or on a slow drive, PoE2 will stutter when it hits the RAM cap:

  • Right-click "This PC" → Properties → Advanced system settings → Performance → Settings → Advanced → Virtual memory → Change
  • Uncheck "Automatically manage paging file size for all drives"
  • Select your SSD (not HDD) → Custom size → Set Initial and Maximum to 16384 MB (16 GB)
  • Click Set, then OK, and reboot

A manually sized 16 GB page file on your fastest SSD ensures that if PoE2 ever exceeds your RAM budget, the spillover goes to fast storage rather than causing a hard stutter. This is especially important for 16 GB systems running juiced maps.

RAM is the hidden foundation of PoE2 performance. If your system has 16 GB and you experience stuttering in endgame, upgrading to 32 GB will deliver a more noticeable improvement than upgrading your GPU in many scenarios.
